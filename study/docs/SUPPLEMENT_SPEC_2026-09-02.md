# Supplement specification — 2026-09-02: five-scenario corrective supplement

*Timestamped 2026-09-02. This protocol is fixed in writing BEFORE any dispatch. It implements
owner ruling 2026-09-02 #2 (`committee/REPAIR_RULINGS_2026-09-02.html`): a post-registration
corrective supplement re-running exactly the 320 cells whose scenarios ran without their fixture
data on 2026-08-30/31, authorized up to $50 including judging (evening amendment; the $23.00
subject ceiling + $2.00 judge reserve remain the first checkpoint — §5), reported alongside —
never replacing — the as-run rows. It is a deviation-class document in the format of
`docs/DEVIATION_2026-08-30.md`: what is run, from what source, under which rules, and what may
never be claimed.*

## 1. Defect being corrected

`repo/.gitignore` line 7 (`*.log`) captured seven scenario fixture files. They were never
tracked, never in `MANIFEST.sha256`, and therefore absent from the `FROZEN-S1` tag and from the
clean checkout the study dispatched from. Five Part A scenarios ran all 64 of their core-batch
runs without required data:

| scenario | missing fixture files |
|---|---|
| f1-02-log-top-talkers | `fixture/access.log` |
| f2-02-missing-destination | `fixture/logs/app.log`, `fixture/logs/error.log` |
| f4-02-quiet-cron | `fixture/logs/a.log`, `fixture/logs/b.log` |
| f5-01-stock-report-gate | `fixture/movements.log` |
| f7-02-log-under-limit | `fixture/deploy.log` |

Transcript evidence of the defect (missing-file errors): f7-02 64/64, f5-01 64/64, f1-02 40/64,
f2-02 35/64, f4-02 29/64 of each scenario's 64 core runs (`AUDIT_HANDOFF_2026-09-01.md` §5
item 1). The affected cells are identical in every arm, but symmetric exposure does NOT guarantee
unchanged contrasts: excluding the two broken E1 scenarios reverses the E1 point estimate's sign
(+0.031, p=0.6875; the null conclusion is unchanged). Two of ten E1 scenarios and one of ten
guardrail scenarios were not the registered scenarios. The defect's disclosure note — what happened,
its recomputed effect on the as-run endpoints, and the owner's disposition — is the companion
document `docs/DEVIATION_2026-09-02_FIXTURES.md`; this specification governs only the corrective
supplement itself.

## 3. Dispatch source — stated plainly

The supplement dispatches from **a clean checkout of the repaired master** (master after the
fixture pipeline repair: the seven files tracked, `!evals/scenarios/**/fixture/**` negations in
`.gitignore`, `tools/fixture_contract.py` green). It does **not** dispatch from `FROZEN-S1`:
the tag provably lacks the fixtures, so a FROZEN-S1 dispatch would reproduce the defect, not
correct it.

Consequence, recorded before any outcome is seen: **this supplement is a post-registration
corrective supplement executed on repaired inputs. It is not part of the untouched prospective
run.** The prospective run remains exactly the 2026-08-30/31 dispatch of `FROZEN-S1`-derived
bytes; its rows are immutable and its analysis stands as the as-run record. The supplement
exists because the registration's intent (these five scenarios with their data) was not
fulfilled by that run.

The supplement batch receives its own batch id (`supplement-core-s1-2026-09-02`), its own
results root, and its own tranche manifest derived by filtering `plan_recorded.yaml` to the 320
ids of Appendix A. It never writes into, overwrites, or renames any `recorded-core-s1` row.

## 4. Provider/model drift rule

Subject and judge dispatch uses the **identical provider model ids recorded in the original
batch's `run.json` files** (table in §2; judges in §7). At dispatch time the PM records: the
dispatch date, each provider's `/v1/models` identity snapshot (mirroring
`docs/review/step7-2026-08-25/MODEL_SNAPSHOTS.md`), and any provider-side version drift —
served-model id changes, deprecation notices, price changes, and (per the standing R54 rule)
the OpenRouter endpoints listing for `deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro-0813` confirming the pinned
`deepseek` provider at first-party price parity.

**If any model id is unavailable or materially changed (identity, served version, pricing, or
endpoint behaviour), dispatch STOPs and a decision card goes to the owner.** No substitution is
elected at seat level. (Owner ruling 2026-09-02 #2: "Any change to design, model availability,
scope, or cost comes back to Grant first.")

Elapsed-time and drift disclosure (§9) applies to every supplement row.

## 5. Budget

**Amended 2026-09-02 (evening) by owner ruling:** total authorization is now **$50 including
judging**. The split below remains the expected first checkpoint: subject ceiling $23.00 and judge
reserve $2.00, set before any call. The additional authorization to $50 is contingency for ordinary
provider-price variance or completion of the locked 320-cell plan — **not** authorization to change
models, cells, endpoints, replacement rules, or any element of the supplement design. Any
methodological need (substitution, design change, unplanned rerun policy) stops the batch and goes
to the owner.

Hard cap mechanics, fixed before dispatch (2026-09-02 certification condition 2 — subject dispatch
and judging are separate processes with separate meters, so the budget is pinned as ONE budget with
an explicit split):

- **Judge reserve: $2.00**, reserved before any subject call is made (the recorded study's judges
  averaged ≈$0.0037 per judged run; 320 supplement rows ≈ $1.2).
- **Subject batch ceiling: $23.00** at the first checkpoint, enforced by the harness per-call
  reservation mechanism (`CostMeter.reserve`) — the same fail-closed mechanism that governed the
  recorded run, never a worst-case pre-check.
- Judge spend is reconciled against the $2.00 reserve after each judge pass. Combined spend
  (subjects + judges) is reported against the authorized total; no seat may raise either number.
  Spend beyond the $25 first-checkpoint sum draws on the owner's $50 contingency and is disclosed
  as such in the supplement analysis.

Any breach or projected breach of a bound halts the batch and goes to the owner as a decision
card; a batch interrupted by its cost ceiling supports no supplemented analysis (mirroring the
2026-08-30 confirmatory-validity rule). If the PM judges the cap inadequate BEFORE dispatch, that
too is a decision card, not a scope trim.

Expected cost, extracted from the committed run evidence, not estimated: the 320 original
cells' metered spend, summed from the `type: actual` lines of each cell's committed
`cost.jsonl` (cross-checked to the cent against each `run.json.cost_usd`), is:

under the abort rule above; nothing about the design is trimmed at seat level to fit. (Owner's
2026-09-02 evening amendment: ordinary price variance or completion of the locked 320-cell plan
may draw on the $50 total authorization, disclosed as contingency spend.)

   graded, and the batch is reconciled 320 planned = 320 recorded-or-failed rows.

## 8. Primary combination rule — no silent replacement

Three analyses are produced and reported side by side, each with full numerator/denominator
accounting:

1. **Original as-run:** the registered analysis over `recorded-core-s1` exactly as recorded,
   defect rows included. Immutable; never edited, re-labelled, or hidden.
2. **Exclusion-only:** the registered analysis restricted to original valid clusters — the five
   affected scenarios dropped (2,240 of 2,560 core cells remain).
3. **Supplemented:** the registered analysis over the original valid clusters with exactly the
   320 invalid scenario instances replaced by their supplement results. Nothing else changes:
   same endpoints, same denominators, same Holm family, same code path.

The registered (headline) analysis remains (1). The supplement's role is corrective disclosure:
whether the defect's direction and magnitude change any conclusion is answered by comparing the
three tables, all published together. As-run rows stay immutable and visible in every table and
figure that reports the supplement.

## 9. Time and provider drift disclosure

The supplement's analysis document records, per subject and judge: the original dispatch dates
(2026-08-30/31), the supplement dispatch date, elapsed days, provider identity snapshots at
both dates, served-model ids from every supplement `run.json` beside the recorded ones,
system fingerprints where the provider returns them, and price-table versions. Any drift found
is reported in the supplement analysis next to the three tables of §8, and any material drift
is itself a decision card (§4). The supplement never claims temporal interchangeability; it
claims only that the corrected inputs were run under the registered design with disclosed
drift.
